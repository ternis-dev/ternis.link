Video links are temporal: the point is at minute 4, second 12 — not "the video". [Generic shorteners fumble this](/pages/blog/2026-10-04-why-video-links-need-a-dedicated-shortener): stripped timestamps, ad-wall interstitials, slugs nobody can read aloud. **href.yt** exists to fix exactly that, and an account turns it from a quick cutter into a creator toolkit.

## The workflow

**1. Cut timestamp-safe links.** Paste a `?t=1m42s` URL and href.yt preserves the offset through the redirect — viewers land on the exact frame. The focused workspace at `href.yt/new` skips every distraction.

**2. Claim vanity slugs.** Guests get auto-generated codes; members pick their own. `href.yt/live`, `href.yt/merch`, `href.yt/ep42` — slugs that fit in a Twitch Nightbot command, an OBS lower-third, or a spoken podcast outro. The rules for slugs that stick are the same as everywhere: short, pronounceable, on-brand ([guide](/pages/blog/2026-09-28-custom-slugs-that-stick)).

**3. Read retention signals.** Which episode link got clicked from the YouTube description versus Discord? What share of the audience is mobile? Per-link analytics answer that without cookies or viewer profiling — aggregate referrers, countries, and browsers, nothing more.

**4. Manage it all in one place.** Every `href.yt` link you make signed in lands on **[my.href.nz](https://my.href.nz/)** alongside your `href.nz` and `meinlink.at` links — the [dedicated public dashboard](/pages/blog/2026-10-05-two-dashboards-public-links-vs-personal-links). Edit destinations, set expiry on sponsor links, export CSVs for sponsor reports, download QR codes for printed merch tables.

Guests are welcome for one-offs. Creators who publish weekly will feel the account pay for itself by the second episode.
