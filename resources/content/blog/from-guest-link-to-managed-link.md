Shortening a link on `href.nz` as a guest takes ten seconds: paste, shorten, copy. No account, no password, an auto-generated 8-character slug, and a fair-use quota of 50 links a day. For one-off shares, that is the whole product — and it stays exactly as is.

But the moment you shorten your tenth link to the same campaign, or someone asks "how many people clicked that?", the guest deal runs out. That is where an account changes the equation.

## What signing in unlocks

Guests get auto-made codes. Members get control:

- **[Custom slugs](/pages/blog/2026-09-28-custom-slugs-that-stick)** — pick memorable codes in your own words instead of random characters.
- **Click analytics** — referrers, countries, browsers, and per-day charts for every link, the same numbers the API serves.
- **QR codes** — print-ready SVG and PNG for any short link ([how they work](/pages/blog/2026-09-28-qr-codes-for-every-short-link)).
- **CSV export** — your whole catalog or per-link click history, for deeper digging.
- **Link management** — descriptions, [tags](/pages/blog/2026-09-29-organize-links-with-tags), expiry dates, password protection, and deactivation without losing stats.

Sign-in runs through Ternis Auth — no local passwords, one account across the network.

## Where it all lives

Your managed public links live on **[my.href.nz](https://my.href.nz/)**: overview stats, the link table, create and edit pages, analytics, and export. It covers `href.nz`, `meinlink.at`, and `href.yt` links in one place — the [split from the personal dashboard](/pages/blog/2026-10-05-two-dashboards-public-links-vs-personal-links) keeps the guest-fast workflow separate from family, business, and newsletter tooling.

Two practical notes. First, links you made as a guest stay guest links — signing in doesn't retroactively attach analytics to them, so start signed in when a link matters. Second, the daily guest quota is per IP: if you hit the 50-link ceiling regularly, that is the product telling you it is time for an account.

Paste the next link the usual way. If it turns out to matter, you'll know where it lives.
